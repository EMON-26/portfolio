Research Plan

Research Statement

My research focuses on building reliable and interpretable machine learning systems for low-resource, code-mixed language settings, particularly Bangla-English social media text. Through my undergraduate thesis, I investigated mental health classification and severity estimation using transformer-based multi-task learning. I am especially interested in methods that balance predictive performance with transparent model behavior, so that AI systems can support responsible human decision making in sensitive domains such as mental health.

In graduate study, I aim to extend this direction by exploring robust language representations, multilingual adaptation, and explainability techniques for practical healthcare and social-impact applications, with outputs suitable for conference publication and collaborative lab projects.

Research Proposal Abstract

Compact abstract format suitable for scholarship forms and supervisor outreach emails.

This proposed Master's research investigates robust and interpretable NLP methods for Bangla-English code-mixed text, with a primary case study in mental health signal classification from social media data. The project will evaluate transformer adaptation strategies for low-resource settings, compare multi-task and single-task learning objectives, and integrate explainability techniques to support trustworthy predictions. Expected outputs include reproducible experimental pipelines, publishable empirical results, and practical guidance for responsible deployment in socially sensitive domains.

Research Experience

Bachelor's Thesis

Banglish Mental Health Classification using Transformer-Based Multi-Task Learning (2025 - Present).

Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Green University of Bangladesh

  • Constructed and annotated a large Banglish mental health dataset covering six classes and three severity levels.
  • Designed a BanglaBERT-based multi-task architecture with Attention Pooling for joint classification and severity prediction.
  • Applied Grad x Input explainability to analyze model reasoning and improve interpretability.
  • Achieved 91.4% accuracy (Macro-F1: 0.918) for class prediction and 92.2% accuracy (Macro-F1: 0.926) for severity estimation.
